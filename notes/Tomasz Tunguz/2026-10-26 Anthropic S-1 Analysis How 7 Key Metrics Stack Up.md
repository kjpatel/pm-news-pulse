# Anthropic S-1 Analysis: How 7 Key Metrics Stack Up

**Source**: [Tomasz Tunguz](https://tomtunguz.com/anthropics-s1-grew-eleven-fold/)
**Author**: Tomasz Tunguz | **Date**: Oct 26, 2026

---

## Summary

Anthropic's S-1 shows explosive revenue growth ($4.6B in 2025, $16.3B in the first half of 2026) and a turn to operating profit, but the case against rests on $637B of mostly non-cancelable compute commitments against consumption-based revenue with no lock-in. The author argues the filing supports both a very large valuation and real risk.

## Key Takeaways

- **Derive gross margin yourself when companies stop reporting it.** Anthropic changed to a nature-of-expense presentation, so the author rebuilt a roughly 40% FY2025 gross margin from the cost-of-revenue line in the notes. Pull the underlying cost lines before trusting headline margins.
- **Separate GAAP from adjusted operating income and check the exclusions.** Anthropic's Q2 2026 operating income was an 8.4% GAAP margin versus 19% adjusted, and FY2025 showed an $8.1B GAAP loss against a $4.9B adjusted loss. Stock comp, settlements, and charitable contributions drive much of that gap.
- **Treat compute commitments as the real leverage.** With no meaningful drawn debt, the risk sits in $637B of commitments, of which about $265.6B were fixed or minimum guarantees at June 30, 2026. Map each contract's cancellation terms, true-up clauses, and delivery-based fees before judging solvency.
- **Watch customer concentration and retention trends together.** Top-customer concentration fell from 76% of revenue from $100K+ accounts in 2025 to a top five at about 25% in 2026, and net revenue retention is reported near 900%. Track whether expansion holds as usage-based revenue scales.
- **Price cost cuts as a growth lever.** Anthropic cut typical workload costs by about 25% and per-task costs by about 40% with new model releases, and revenue growth accelerated after an earlier cost-per-task reduction. For AI products, lowering unit cost can expand demand faster than it erodes price.
