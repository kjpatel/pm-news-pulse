# The Four Questions of AI

**Source**: [Tomasz Tunguz](https://tomtunguz.com/the-four-questions-of-ai/)
**Author**: Tomasz Tunguz | **Date**: Nov 02, 2026

---

## Summary

AI is best understood as a four-layer stack (application, harness and context, model, data center), with each layer facing an unresolved economic question that couples to the others. Because the layers interact directly, shifts in one, such as GPU financing costs or inference co-design, can reshape economics across the whole stack.

## Key Takeaways

- **Treat the harness as a margin lever:** A well-designed harness that manages context can cut the cost of the same answer substantially (cited as 71%) and raise gross margin on AI contracts, so harness design belongs in the business model, not just the engineering roadmap.
- **Build a context layer you control:** Enterprises should own memory and permissions in-house so they can swap models underneath without shipping sensitive data out in prompts.
- **Route tasks to the right model size:** Most agent tasks can run on smaller or fine-tuned domain models at a fraction of frontier cost, so match each task to the cheapest model that meets quality needs.
- **Watch the credit and power constraints:** Rising concrete, turbine, and grid costs, along with widening credit default spreads, mean data center capacity and financing may limit AI growth more than model capability does.
- **Stress-test infrastructure assumptions:** Question whether GPUs last five years, whether cash flows match twenty-year debt, and whether GPU-hour prices will rise or fall before committing capital or pricing long-term contracts.
