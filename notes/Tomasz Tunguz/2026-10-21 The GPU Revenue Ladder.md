# The GPU Revenue Ladder

**Source**: [Tomasz Tunguz](https://tomtunguz.com/the-gpu-revenue-ladder/)
**Author**: Tomasz Tunguz | **Date**: Oct 21, 2026

---

## Summary

SpaceX's Q3 report and DeepSeek's inference disclosure show that large-scale GPU inference is a healthy business, with prices across four measures ranging from $2.44 to $38 per card-hour. The author argues that rising GPU rental prices and a supply pipeline that is largely unbuilt leave room for prices to climb, though falling token prices and on-device inference are real counterweights.

## Key Takeaways

- **Model GPU economics on realized revenue, not theoretical maximums.** DeepSeek's $38 per card-hour assumes perfect utilization and full list prices, while SpaceX's realized figure is $2.44. Use the realized number as the baseline when evaluating inference business plans.
- **Check payback claims for pre- or after-tax basis before relying on them.** The 'under a year' payback becomes roughly 1.2 years on a cash basis, and bonus depreciation can shorten it further without improving net present value. Ask any vendor or executive which basis they use.
- **Track the gap between token deflation and GPU rental prices.** GPU rental rates doubled in six months while the cost of a fixed benchmark fell 377-fold. Gross profit per card-hour depends on throughput gains outpacing price cuts, so measure throughput per GPU each quarter.
- **Watch on-device inference as a demand threat to cloud providers.** Qualcomm says some AI companies want 100-billion-parameter models running on phones by 2028. Plan for workloads that may shift off the cloud, especially latency-tolerant or privacy-sensitive ones.
- **Factor data center delivery delays into capacity planning.** JPMorgan reports over 60% of 2027 planned capacity is not yet under construction, and Goldman puts on-time delivery at 50% to 60%. Secure power and capacity commitments early rather than assuming hardware will be deployable on schedule.
