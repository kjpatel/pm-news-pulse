# Crossing the Trillion Dollar Chasm

**Source**: [Tomasz Tunguz](https://tomtunguz.com/crossing-the-trillion-dollar-chasm/)
**Author**: Tomasz Tunguz | **Date**: Oct 29, 2026

---

## Summary

The article argues that the first $10 trillion company will be the one whose new AI compute business reaches $1 trillion in annualized revenue before AI erodes the legacy profit pools that fund its GPU buildout. It models cloud growth trajectories to show Google Cloud and SpaceX could reach that mark around 2030 and AWS around 2032, if power, silicon, and balance sheets hold.

## Key Takeaways

- **Separate segment revenue from consolidated revenue when valuing AI targets.** Jassy's $1T goal applies to AWS segment revenue ($184B annualized), not Amazon's $808B consolidated figure, so compare like with like before judging a target.
- **Track whether legacy cash cows can fund the capex cycle.** Watch Search and advertising growth as the test case: Google Search growth slowed from 19% to 12% as AI Mode monetized below classic search, which is the signal investors are pricing.
- **Check whether AI query monetization matches legacy yield.** AI Mode queries run about 3x longer than traditional searches but monetize at a lower rate, so measure revenue per query rather than query volume when assessing AI products.
- **Model compute constraints against revenue targets.** SpaceX's $500B to $1T AI ARR goal implies 28 to 56 GW of live compute versus roughly 2 GW of annual build capacity, so test whether power and capex can physically support a growth plan.
- **Watch seat-based businesses for AI erosion.** Microsoft is expanding ARPU through AI bundles, while ServiceNow's market cap fell 29% on fears that AI would erode its seat-based model, so assess how AI changes your own pricing unit.
