# No Shirt, No Shoes, No Service for Bots

**Source**: [Tomasz Tunguz](https://tomtunguz.com/no-shirt-no-shoes-no-service-for-bots/)
**Author**: Tomasz Tunguz | **Date**: Nov 13, 2026

---

## Summary

Large consumer storefronts like United Airlines and Amazon are blocking AI shopping agents because their profits depend on ads, upgrades, and fees layered on loss-leader base products, while Shopify welcomes agents because it earns a cut of every transaction regardless of buyer type. The author argues that merchants must re-price their funnels before the bot ban can end.

## Key Takeaways

- **Audit where your profit actually comes from.** Separate base-product margin from ads, upgrades, warranties, and fees to see whether agent buyers would erode your real earnings.
- **Recognize that agents skip high-margin add-ons.** An AI buyer on a mission declines sponsored listings, upsells, and extended warranties, so models built on attachments lose their revenue when agents shop.
- **Consider transaction-based monetization.** Platforms like Shopify that take a percentage of every payment collect the same fee from humans and agents, making them structurally agent-friendly.
- **Plan an explicit agent checkout pricing strategy.** Options include raising base prices, charging a clear agent checkout fee, or designing incentives that work on bots, rather than blocking them.
- **Adopt open agent standards early.** Shopify's WebMCP integration let browser agents complete checkout faster and cheaper than browser automation, showing that agent-native infrastructure can beat scraping-based approaches.
