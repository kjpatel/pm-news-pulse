# When the Central Bank of AI Changes Chairs

**Source**: [Tomasz Tunguz](https://tomtunguz.com/the-impact-of-jensens-resignation/)
**Author**: Tomasz Tunguz | **Date**: Nov 12, 2026

---

## Summary

Jensen Huang's resignation as Nvidia CEO leaves CFO Colette Kress running a company that has become the central financier of AI, through equity stakes, M&A, and lease and capacity guarantees. The article argues that any tightening of Nvidia's capital commitments could slow the AI buildout, and that the leadership change adds uncertainty for startups and open-source model builders.

## Key Takeaways

- **Stress-test startup exposure to Nvidia's capital.** Map which of your funding, compute, or customer relationships depend on Nvidia's equity checks, lease backstops, or extended payment terms, and plan for slower deals if those tighten.
- **Diversify compute suppliers now.** Nvidia's leadership transition and the rise of custom ASICs (Google TPUs, AWS Trainium, Meta MTIA) make single-vendor dependence a real risk; benchmark alternative accelerators for your inference workloads.
- **Plan for open-model funding gaps.** With Nvidia no longer the dominant patron of open-source AI, teams building on open-weight models should identify backup sources of training compute and capital.
- **Watch for export-control changes.** Expect more pressure for stricter semiconductor rules, so audit your hardware supply chain and compliance posture before new requirements arrive.
- **Read the cash flow, not just the headlines.** Track vendors' operating cash flow against their equity and M&A outflows, along with receivables and DSO, to judge whether a supplier's ecosystem commitments are sustainable.
