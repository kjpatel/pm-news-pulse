# The 10x Wall

**Source**: [Tomasz Tunguz](https://tomtunguz.com/the-10x-wall/)
**Author**: Tomasz Tunguz | **Date**: Nov 20, 2026

---

## Summary

Anthropic and SpaceX trade at the same 9.5x forward revenue multiple despite growing 288% and 466%, showing that above $1 trillion of enterprise value, public markets stop paying for top-line growth and anchor valuations to a 20x to 26x earnings band. Future returns at this scale will depend on growing into earnings rather than on multiple expansion.

## Key Takeaways

- **Value mega-cap AI companies on earnings, not revenue growth.** Above $1 trillion of enterprise value, forward sales multiples cap near 10x, so model returns from future earnings and margins rather than assuming multiple expansion.
- **Compare AI businesses on forward EV/sales alongside growth and margin.** Anthropic and SpaceX both sit at 9.5x despite very different growth (288% vs. 466%) and gross margins (22% vs. 62%), so check whether the multiple reflects the metrics that matter for your thesis.
- **Use P/E as the ceiling check on revenue multiples.** Work backward from a 20x to 26x earnings band and the company's net margin to estimate the maximum sustainable revenue multiple, as the author does for Microsoft, Meta, and Google.
- **Read S-1 footnotes and filings for the underlying data.** The article's figures depend on specific sources such as Koyfin, S&P Global, and SEC 10-Q filings with defined periods, so verify trailing and forward estimates before drawing conclusions.
- **Treat the gross margin gap as a key diligence question.** Anthropic's 22% trailing gross margin versus SpaceX's 62% is a large difference at similar valuations, so assess whether compute costs will compress or expand margins as revenue scales.
