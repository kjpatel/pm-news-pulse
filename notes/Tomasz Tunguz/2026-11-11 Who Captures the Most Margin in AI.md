# Who Captures the Most Margin in AI?

**Source**: [Tomasz Tunguz](https://tomtunguz.com/who-captures-the-most-margin-in-ai/)
**Author**: Tomasz Tunguz | **Date**: Nov 11, 2026

---

## Summary

Using public filings from CoreWeave, Anthropic, and data center landlords, the article traces AI's gross profit across the stack from power plant to agent, measured in dollars per watt per year. At a 40% application margin, applications, neoclouds, and inference vendors capture about 92% of the gross profit pool before GPU depreciation.

## Key Takeaways

- **Model your margin per watt, not per token or per GPU-hour.** Normalizing each layer's pricing to $/w/yr makes it possible to compare utilities, data centers, neoclouds, inference, and applications on one scale.
- **Test whether your product can pass compute cost increases to customers.** With new multi-year neocloud contracts moving to $20 to $25 per watt per year versus a $9 blended fleet rate, applications that can't raise prices will be squeezed.
- **Price on the ROI your product delivers, not on cost.** A tool that saves 70% of work supports far more pricing power than one that saves 30%, so quantify and document the savings your customers see.
- **Use small models, fine-tuning, and routing to protect gross margin.** These techniques lower the compute cost per task, which matters most when upstream prices are rising.
- **Watch for vertical integration in your market.** Providers are buying software layers like training, evaluation, and orchestration, so a standalone vendor should plan for the margin pressure and acquisition risk this creates.
