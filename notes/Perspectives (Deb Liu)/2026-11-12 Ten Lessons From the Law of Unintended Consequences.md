# Ten Lessons From the Law of Unintended Consequences

**Source**: [Perspectives (Deb Liu)](https://debliu.substack.com/p/ten-lessons-from-the-law-of-unintended)
**Author**: Deb Liu | **Date**: Nov 12, 2026

---

## Summary

Well-intentioned rules often produce second-order effects that hurt the people they were meant to help, because people respond to incentives. Using examples such as elevator, fire-exit, and parking requirements and rent control, the article argues that policymakers should stay humble, watch the actual results, and adjust.

## Key Takeaways

- **Map second-order effects before writing a rule.** Ask not only what the rule is meant to do, but how people, builders, and markets will respond to it.
- **Check whether a safety or accessibility standard is making the outcome worse.** Compare the cost of the standard with the number of people it actually helps, as with elevators and two-staircase requirements, and consider lower-cost alternatives such as a simple elevator or a single stair with sprinklers.
- **Price in hidden costs that are spread across everyone.** Items like mandatory parking or rent-floor standards can raise costs for people who never use them, so count those costs explicitly in any tradeoff analysis.
- **Build in regular reviews and adjustment.** Treat rules as living systems, collect data on their effects after they take hold, and revise them when the results differ from the intent.
- **Stay curious and humble when proposing changes.** Assume your fix may create a new problem, and test it on a small scale before committing to it broadly.
