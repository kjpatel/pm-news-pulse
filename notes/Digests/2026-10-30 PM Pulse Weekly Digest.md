### 1. [The Horizon Gap: Why GTM and Product Keep Missing Each Other](https://runthebusiness.substack.com/p/the-horizon-gap-why-gtm-and-product)
*Run the Business* — Ibrahim Bashir — Oct 27, 2026  `#Roadmapping`  `#Product Growth`

Bashir describes a common problem where product leaders newly co-own revenue and retention targets but struggle to connect their work to those outcomes. The article offers practical tips for bridging the gap between product and go-to-market teams. Its focus on shared goals maps directly to growth-stage roadmap and PLG expansion planning.

**Why it matters**: Directly addresses a VP of Product's need to align roadmap with shared revenue and retention targets alongside GTM.

- **Agree on shared revenue and retention metrics before roadmap debates.** Define which numbers product and GTM both own, and who is accountable for each, so prioritization starts from the same scoreboard.
- **Map the handoffs between product and GTM across the customer lifecycle.** Identify where activation, expansion, and churn move from marketing and sales into product, and where they move back, then assign owners to each handoff.
- **Build a regular review cadence that uses shared dashboards.** Hold a recurring meeting where product and GTM look at the same funnel and retention data together, rather than reporting on their own separate views.
- **Use a shared time horizon for planning.** Align product roadmap milestones with the sales cycle and renewal timing so that features land when GTM can actually use them to move revenue or retention.
- **Treat customer feedback from GTM as product input.** Set up a regular channel where account and sales teams surface churn reasons and deal blockers directly to product, and track whether those signals change what gets built.

### 2. [Why Reddit built its own AI prototyping tool | Blair Metcalf](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/why-reddit-built-its-own-ai-prototyping)
*Lenny's Newsletter* — Lenny Rachitsky (featuring Blair Metcalf) — Oct 26, 2026  `#AI Tools`  `#Design`

Metcalf explains how Reddit built Reddit Proto, an in-house vibe-coding harness connected to a custom design system MCP. The episode argues that grounding prototypes in real data and design-system mappings can produce production-ready output and near-universal adoption across product and design. For a VP of Product, it offers a concrete pattern for scaling AI-assisted building across the org.

**Why it matters**: Shows how a company-wide prototyping harness with a design-system MCP drove near-universal adoption, a model for scaling product practice.

- **Build prototyping on your own design system.** Create an MCP server that maps your product's components and language to production code so AI-generated prototypes match what engineers will ship.
- **Prototype with real production data, not mock data.** Real Reddit data surfaces edge cases and realistic behavior that mock data hides, making prototype feedback more trustworthy.
- **Make targeted edits by annotating the prototype.** Mark up a specific element and send that annotation to Cursor as a scoped code change instead of re-prompting the whole prototype.
- **Hand off work as a feature capsule.** Package each prototype's scope, components, and behavior into a self-contained unit so engineers can pick up implementation without reverse-engineering the prototype.
- **Create a 'Rosetta stone' to translate between design and code.** Map terminology and components across tools so designers, PMs, and engineers share one vocabulary when working with AI.

### 3. [Defining What Good Looks Likes - All Things Product Podcast with Teresa Torres & Petra Wille](https://www.producttalk.org/defining-what-good-looks-likes-all-things-product-podcast-with-teresa-torres-petra-wille/)
*Product Talk* — Teresa Torres (featuring Petra Wille) — Oct 27, 2026  `#AI Strategy`  `#Metrics`

Torres and Wille argue AI evals are not a new kind of problem but the same work product teams have always done in defining what good looks like. They recommend measuring customer and business value, not only technical performance. They also urge teams to define quality in their own context rather than outsourcing it to generic evals.

**Why it matters**: Reframes AI evals as the familiar work of defining quality and outcomes, useful for integrating AI features into product strategy.

- **Define "good" in your own context.** Write down what a successful customer outcome looks like for your product instead of relying on off-the-shelf eval suites, since the right answer depends on who the user is.
- **Build evals across three layers.** Measure technology performance, customer value, and business value together, so you don't stop at uptime, speed, or whether an answer was concise.
- **Treat "good" as the customer creating value.** Identify the specific action a customer takes that creates value (for example, sending a document in DocuSign) and measure whether users reach it, rather than tracking vanity metrics like MAU.
- **Let context change the definition.** Test whether a response fits the user's level, since a concise answer may be right for an expert and wrong for a beginner.
- **State your expectations before you test.** Write down what you expect to happen so you can recognize and learn from being wrong, and fix the underlying product or process before automating it with AI.

**4.** [Interview with Eric Ries: AI, Founder Mode, and Why Good Companies Go Bad](https://itamargilad.com/eric-ries-incorruptible/) — *Itamar Gilad* · Oct 27, 2026  `#Leadership`  `#Startups`

In this interview, Eric Ries argues that AI makes validated learning more important, because cheaper building raises the cost of wrong turns, and that the Lean Startup loop of build, measure, and learn must stay balanced. He also argues that companies can resist short-term financial pressure through governance choices and small "islands of freedom" that protect mission-focused work.

- **Keep the full build-measure-learn loop running.** Faster building doesn't shorten the loop by itself, so teams should check whether they are still measuring and learning from real customers, not just shipping features.
- **Don't replace real customers with simulated ones.** Using AI-generated feedback as a stand-in for talking to or selling to actual customers is risky. Validate with real users and real purchases.
- **Treat validation as building real things, not as separate research.** Expose real customers to minimum viable products early instead of running a research phase before building.
- **Start change with small islands of freedom.** When the wider organization is dysfunctional, a few committed people can build a protected bubble, then grow it by pulling in middle managers tied to the specific project.
- **Redefine value beyond profit.** Look for hidden assets such as trustworthiness, integrity, safety, and open source, which can strengthen morale, innovation, and long-term value without changing the business model.

**8.** [How to steal your way to a hit product, with Mark Pincus (founder, Zynga)](https://www.news.aakashg.com/p/how-to-steal-your-way-to-a-hit-product) — *Product Growth* · Oct 26, 2026  `#Discovery`  `#Product Growth`

Mark Pincus argues that every great product starts as a copy, and that the skill lies in separating proven elements worth copying exactly from the single new idea a launch should test. He adds that early in a platform shift, teams should ship less new and validate demand before building the full product.

- **Copy what's proven click by click:** Map the closest competitor's experience at a granular level and replicate every element that already works for your audience on your platform, so your launch only measures your one novel idea.
- **Apply the 10-out-of-10 test before calling anything 'better':** Only label an idea better if 10 of 10 users of the existing product would agree it is better; otherwise classify it as new and treat it as an experiment.
- **Limit new features early in a platform shift:** When the market is still forming, prioritize improving proven patterns with AI rather than inventing new features, and write down which part of your AI product is truly new so you aren't running multiple experiments in one launch.
- **Test demand before building the product:** Use a teardown, concept batch, and a one-page landing site with paid ads to measure click-through rate against a bar you set in advance, before committing to a full MVP.
- **Plan for most new ideas to fail:** Assume your own new idea is probably wrong, avoid letting failing ideas linger, and give real reverence to new features that prove themselves in the market, since they then become proven.

Rob Snyder walks through his PULL framework (Project, Unavoidable, List of options, Limitations) for finding product-market fit, from generating an idea to closing the first handful of deals. He argues that real demand, not founder conviction, should drive every step.

- **Map your idea against the PULL framework:** Identify the Project, test whether the problem is Unavoidable for customers, list the alternatives they already use, and state the Limitations of your approach before investing heavily.
- **Skip design partners as a shortcut to validation:** Snyder advises against them, so look for buyers who would pay for the solution rather than customers who are only being polite about feedback.
- **Run cold outreach and sales calls early:** Reach out directly to prospects and run your first sales conversations yourself to learn what actually moves someone to buy.
- **Set a plan for when nothing is working:** Snyder's "goblin mode" idea is to change tactics deliberately when traction stalls, rather than continuing the same approach or giving up.
- **Apply PULL inside larger companies:** Use the same demand-first test for internal projects by checking whether the need is unavoidable for the people you serve, not just whether leadership wants it.
