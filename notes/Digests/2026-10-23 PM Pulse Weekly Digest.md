### 1. [Experimentation, AI and the Product Model](https://www.svpg.com/experimentation-ai-and-the-product-model/)
*SVPG* — Marty Cagan — Oct 21, 2026  `#Roadmapping`  `#Discovery`

Marty Cagan argues that experimentation should be the core competency of product teams within the product operating model, moving beyond optimization toward innovation and outcomes. He contends that as AI makes building cheap, the hard problem becomes deciding what to build. Cagan recommends using experiments to learn before committing engineering effort, which bears directly on how a VP of Product structures discovery and roadmaps.

**Why it matters**: Reframes product teams around experimentation as AI lowers build costs, directly shaping roadmapping and team operating model.

- **Treat experimentation as a discovery tool, not just optimization:** Use qualitative prototypes and rapid tests to learn what to build before committing engineering time, rather than only measuring what was already shipped.
- **Match the experiment type to the work:** Use broad qualitative experiments for new (zero-to-one) products, mixed qualitative and quantitative experiments for improving existing products, and data-driven experiments for optimization.
- **Shift teams from delivering features to owning outcomes:** Give product teams business problems to solve rather than project roadmaps, and use live-data experiments to attribute results to outcomes.
- **Build prototypes to test assumptions before building production software:** With AI cutting build costs, prioritize cheap, fast prototypes that test value, usability, viability, and feasibility first.
- **Include go-to-market and monetization in new product experiments:** For new products, test cost structure, pricing, and go-to-market strategy alongside the standard discovery risks to reach product/market fit.

### 2. [The AI monetization debate shifts again](https://www.growthunhinged.com/p/usage-based-pricing-for-ai)
*Growth Unhinged* — Kyle Poyar — Oct 21, 2026  `#PLG`  `#AI Strategy`

Kyle Poyar reports that AI monetization is moving from flat access fees toward variable pricing based on usage and outcomes, which investors and AI-native companies increasingly favor. He notes that customers still want predictability, so the key go-to-market challenge is making variable pricing feel predictable. For a Series C SaaS leader, this raises concrete questions about packaging, expansion revenue, and how pricing changes affect forecasting.

**Why it matters**: Addresses how AI pricing shifts from seat fees to usage and outcomes, a central PLG and expansion revenue decision.

- **Plan for a cross-functional pricing change:** Moving to usage-based pricing affects billing, sales comp, enablement, product strategy, post-sales investment, and forecasting, so map these dependencies before switching models.
- **Design variable pricing to feel predictable:** Customers equate usage-based pricing with pay-as-you-go and dislike surprise overage bills, so build spend controls, forecasts, or caps into the offer.
- **Use predictability to stabilize revenue:** Making variable pricing feel predictable to customers also makes your own revenue more predictable, which supports forecasting and planning.
- **Expect hybrid models to be common:** In Kyle's 2026 State of Monetization data, 76% of AI-native companies already use variable or hybrid pricing versus 49% of SaaS companies, so consider hybrid structures rather than pure consumption or pure seats.
- **Be cautious with outcome-based pricing:** Metronome's CEO argues outcome pricing is mostly feasible only for companies with monopoly-like power or very large contracts, so most teams should price on countable outputs or units of work instead.

### 3. [The Definitive Report on PM Compensation and Hiring in 2026](https://www.news.aakashg.com/p/product-manager-salary-hiring-report-2026)
*Product Growth* — Aakash Gupta — Oct 22, 2026  `#Hiring`  `#Org Design`

Aakash Gupta reports that PM hiring has fallen sharply since 2022 while AI PM hiring has grown and pay has risen, even as outside hires shrink. He argues job postings overstate market health, so the report tracks actual hires and compensation instead. For a VP of Product planning team growth, the data suggests where to source talent and how to budget for AI-focused PM roles.

**Why it matters**: Provides hiring and compensation data that helps calibrate PM org scaling and headcount plans.

- **Measure the market by hires, not postings.** Postings are up 77% since 2023, but actual outside hires are down 18%, so judge your job search by real placement odds rather than open listings.
- **Track hires per exit to gauge how open the door is.** A ratio near 1.0 means the field is flat; in 2025 PM ran about 0.92 outside hires per exit, while AI PM ran 47% above exits, so target the AI PM title if you want a wider door.
- **Look beyond big tech when searching for PM roles.** Banks and payment networks like Capital One, JPMorgan Chase, and Mastercard hired more PMs than Meta, so widening your target list to financial and payment companies expands your options.
- **Use the AI PM title as a floor, not a ceiling.** Many people doing AI product work don't carry the title, so searching for AI-related PM work and not just the "AI PM" label can reveal more opportunities.
- **Negotiate on pay trends, not hiring trends.** Median PM compensation rose from $220K in 2022 to $250K in 2026 even as US hires fell 48%, so a shrinking market has not reduced what hiring managers pay for experienced PMs.

- **Reserve intense sprints for real deadlines.** Long hours can be justified for launches, emergencies, or rare opportunities, but avoid designing an organization where everyone is expected to sprint indefinitely.
- **Treat rising output expectations as a trap.** When AI makes work faster, resist the urge to simply take on 10 projects instead of 1; decide what productivity gains should buy, such as better quality, more rest, or fewer projects.
- **Broaden your hiring funnel beyond the always-on candidate.** Parents, caregivers, and people with health constraints bring experience and perspectives that a homogeneous team lacks, and they have often already learned from mistakes you are about to make.
- **Make boundaries safe, not just allowed.** If you are a leader, ensure people who protect their time are not penalized, and model healthy schedules yourself so that less privileged or early-career employees can set limits too.
