### 1. [How to Survive AI as a SaaS Company](https://www.news.aakashg.com/p/can-saas-survive-the-ai-era)
*Product Growth* — Aakash Gupta (featuring Linda Tong) — Nov 10, 2026  `#Competitive Strategy`  `#AI Strategy`

Gupta, featuring Linda Tong, argues AI gives customers a free first version of most software, so SaaS companies must build the work that comes after publish. The piece suggests serving a narrow buyer AI cannot reach and treating AI platforms as a distribution channel. It also argues product teams need structure for speed, with every PM acting as an AI PM.

**Why it matters**: Directly addresses how a Series C SaaS company must reposition against AI-generated software and structure PMs as AI PMs.

- **Push up your growth rate to push up your multiple.** Public SaaS companies with multiples above 15x grew a median 34%, versus 11% for those under 5x, so growth is the lever to track and report on.
- **Build the work AI leaves behind.** Identify the post-first-draft tasks (editing, governance, collaboration, publishing, ongoing iteration) that AI tools handle poorly, and make those your core product.
- **Pick one buyer and go deep.** Pull usage data by job-to-be-done, map job titles to each job, and choose the job many titles share as your ICP before expanding to adjacent users.
- **Treat ChatGPT and Claude as your top of funnel.** Make it easy for users to start in an AI site builder or agent and bring the work into your product, then measure success by publish rate and time to first publish.
- **Restructure the team around prototypes and taste.** Have PMs build first prototypes in code, staff small pods of two or three engineers, a designer, and a PM, and measure outcomes over output.

### 2. [The AI monetization debate shifts again](https://www.growthunhinged.com/p/usage-based-pricing-for-ai)
*Growth Unhinged* — Kyle Poyar — Nov 07, 2026  `#PLG`  `#AI Strategy`

Poyar reports AI monetization is shifting from flat fees toward usage and outcome-based pricing, with investors and AI-native companies favoring variable models. He notes customers still want predictability. He argues SaaS teams should make variable pricing feel predictable and ramp adoption before expecting expansion revenue.

**Why it matters**: Gives a practical framework for moving pricing toward usage or outcomes while keeping buyers' predictability, a core PLG and expansion question.

- **Make variable pricing feel predictable**: Buyers fear the first overage bill, so design pricing around predictability first, since this also makes your own revenue more predictable.
- **Consider a universal credit model**: If your product spans several AI actions with different pricing metrics, convert each action into credits that customers can buy in packs and draw down across products, similar to a law firm retainer.
- **Add structural safeguards to your pricing**: Options include hard budget caps at the account and user level, usage tiers with annual drawdown, a platform fee that includes baseline usage, or an adaptive flat rate with annual true-up.
- **Start with a high-value use case and expand gradually**: Usage-based customers have less shelfware tolerance, so nail one high-value use case before rolling out broadly, the way you would scale ad spend after proving ROI.
- **Track time-to-ramp and share of wallet**: Measure how long the average customer takes to reach 80% of their usage allowance, assign an owner to accelerate it, and measure how much addressable spend each account contains to find the expansion ceiling.

### 3. [Personal Agent Systems Explained - How 5 Leaders Run their work through AI Agents](https://departmentofproduct.substack.com/p/personal-agent-systems-explained)
*Department of Product* — Rich Holmes — Nov 12, 2026  `#Agentic`  `#Org Design`

Holmes describes how leaders such as Coinbase's Brian Armstrong, Notion's COO, and Linear's CEO organize work around personal agent systems. Typically a lead agent covers each focus area under a chief-of-staff agent. He argues product teams should start experimenting with building their own versions.

**Why it matters**: Offers a concrete operating model for product leaders to experiment with agent teams, which bears on how to scale a product org.

- **Organize agents by focus area:** Assign one always-running lead agent to each area of your product work, with its own sub-agents for tracing issues, testing, and chasing collaborators.
- **Add a chief of staff agent:** Create an oversight agent that tracks all lead agents and consolidates their findings so you can make decisions in one place instead of context switching.
- **Build a shared context vault:** Keep a central store of your context, such as notes, decisions, and priorities, that every agent you try can plug into.
- **Use adversarial agents for big decisions:** Set up an agent whose job is to challenge your reasoning before major product or business calls.
- **Start with one specific problem:** Pick a single recurring workflow, like handling bug complaints, and build a small personal agent system around it before expanding.
