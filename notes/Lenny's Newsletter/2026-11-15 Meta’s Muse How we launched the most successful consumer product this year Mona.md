## Summary

Mona Sarantakos, VP of Product at Meta Superintelligence Labs, explains how Muse grew from a weekend OpenClaw experiment on a Raspberry Pi into the #1 app in the App Store. She describes why the team waited seven months to launch and how the product's activation levers and user insight process work.

## Key Takeaways

- **Prototype with the tools you already love:** Muse began as a weekend project running OpenClaw on a Raspberry Pi, so start with a quick hands-on experiment before committing to a full product build.
- **Use the launch delay deliberately:** Muse waited seven months to ship despite starting earlier than competitors, so treat timing as a product decision and use the extra time to raise quality and fit before launch.
- **Focus on the two levers that drive activation:** For AI agents, identify the specific actions that move new users to their first meaningful outcome, and design onboarding around those moments.
- **Make the agent feel approachable:** The placeholder avatar Jolly became a beloved icon, which shows that personality and a friendly visual identity can matter more than polish when building consumer AI.
- **Use agent swarms to scale user research:** Deploy agents to analyze and surface user insights across large volumes of conversations, and stay alert to unexpected use cases that the team did not predict.
