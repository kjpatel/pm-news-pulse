# What it actually takes to build frontier models | Tulsee Doshi (Head of Product for Gemini)

**Source**: [Lenny's Newsletter](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/what-it-actually-takes-to-build-frontier)
**Author**: Lenny Rachitsky (featuring Tulsee Doshi) | **Date**: Nov 08, 2026

---

## Summary

Tulsee Doshi, Google's head of product for frontier models including Gemini, explains how building frontier models is closer to a craft than pure engineering, and how Google approaches full-stack frontier AI and the role PMs play inside a frontier lab. The conversation centers on balancing near-term wins against capabilities that are almost ready.

## Key Takeaways

- **Treat model development as a craft, not just engineering.** Doshi compares it to Snape's potions class: small inputs, precise timing, and a lot of judgment that doesn't reduce to a checklist, so PMs should expect ambiguity and iterate on taste as much as on specs.
- **Split your roadmap across what works now and what's almost working.** Her 60-20-20 framework allocates most effort to the current winners, with the remaining portions reserved for near-term bets that are close to working. Apply it to your own roadmap by explicitly labeling each initiative by maturity.
- **Write your first eval before you build more features.** The episode walks through how to write a first eval, so define what good output looks like for your use case, create a small test set, and score model outputs against it before you expand scope.
- **Use full-stack thinking across models, products, and infrastructure.** Google's strategy spans research, models, and product surfaces, so PMs should map how their feature depends on model capability, serving infrastructure, and distribution, and identify where a gap in one layer blocks progress in another.
- **Invest in becoming a stronger PM as AI raises the bar.** Doshi argues great PMs are becoming more valuable in frontier labs, so focus on judgment about user needs, prioritization under uncertainty, and communicating tradeoffs to technical teams.
