# Report: What people *really* think about IC work.

**Source**: [Elena's Growth Scoop](https://www.elenaverna.com/p/report-what-people-really-think-about)
**Author**: Elena Verna | **Date**: Aug 13, 2026

---

## Summary

A comprehensive research study reveals that most people, including managers, prefer individual contributor (IC) work over management roles when compensation and prestige are equal, with 71% wanting at least half their time spent on IC tasks. The primary trade-off for IC roles is influence over company strategy rather than compensation, with 66% of respondents citing reduced organizational influence as the main downside.

## Key Takeaways

- **Shift IC recruitment messaging** - Stop selling IC roles on technical depth alone; emphasize autonomy, hands-on impact, and freedom from meetings, as 27% of respondents want 100% IC work and 71% want at least half their time in IC roles.
- **Reframe influence as currency** - Address the influence gap by creating formal mechanisms for ICs to shape strategy (advisory boards, design reviews, cross-functional leadership), since 66% cite reduced influence as the main downside of IC work, not compensation.
- **Redesign manager incentives** - Current managers significantly overestimate how much politics and lost hands-on work bother them compared to actual experience; use this insight to redesign manager roles to include more IC-style work (player-coach model) to improve satisfaction.
- **Target manager-to-IC transitions** - 75%+ of former-manager ICs wouldn't trade their roles for anyone else in the org versus only 30% of current managers, indicating this transition can create highly satisfied, high-impact employees.
- **Create hybrid 'player-coach' paths** - Develop clear pathways for managers to split time between people leadership and hands-on IC work, as this model appeals to those seeking both influence and direct impact.
