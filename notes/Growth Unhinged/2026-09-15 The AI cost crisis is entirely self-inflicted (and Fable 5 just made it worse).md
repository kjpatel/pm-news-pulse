# The AI cost crisis is entirely self-inflicted (and Fable 5 just made it worse)

**Source**: [Growth Unhinged](https://www.growthunhinged.com/p/the-ai-cost-crisis-is-entirely-self-inflicted-and-fable-5-just-made-it-worse)
**Author**: Kyle Poyar (featuring Marty Kausas, John McCauley, Dan Zhang, Sonalee Parekh) | **Date**: Sep 15, 2026

---

## Summary

The article argues that AI cost blowups, such as Pylon's projected jump from $400K to $1.4M a year, stem from companies rushing into AI adoption without tracking spend or matching models to tasks. It contends that costs will keep rising unless GTM teams manage usage deliberately without abandoning adoption.

## Key Takeaways

- **Track AI spend by individual and team before setting limits.** Pylon discovered that one engineer's Claude usage ran about $3,100 a month and that a single ad hoc analysis cost $4,000, so visibility comes before any cost decisions.
- **Set cheaper default models for everyday work.** Vanta moved routine tasks to Sonnet 4.6 and blocked Fable, since Opus 4.7 used far more tokens per task at a similar per-token price.
- **Split workloads across providers to avoid enterprise tier cliffs.** Pylon's jump came from crossing 150 seats, which moved them to Anthropic's Enterprise tier with no token subsidy, so spreading functions across vendors can keep usage under those thresholds.
- **Use automation instead of AI reasoning where possible.** Pairing AI with deterministic automation is more reliable and costs a fraction as much, and per-ticket investigations that cost $2 each are a good place to start.
- **Batch low-urgency work and cap output tokens.** Anthropic's Batch API offers 50% off, and capping output or testing on a small scale prevents large tasks from silently burning credits.
