# What AI agents really think about your pricing

**Source**: [Growth Unhinged](https://www.growthunhinged.com/p/ai-pricing-visibility-data)
**Author**: Kyle Poyar (featuring Nikolas Laskaris) | **Date**: Oct 23, 2026

---

## Summary

AI engines are now a major channel where B2B buyers learn about pricing, but they often give inaccurate or negative answers, and company pricing pages are rarely the first source they cite. The article argues that companies win by making pricing readable to AI and building a broader 'pricing answer stack' across third-party sources.

## Key Takeaways

- **Test your pricing page like a bot**: Curl the page or disable JavaScript to see what crawlers actually receive, and check for client-side rendering, interactive tabs or sliders, robots.txt blocks, and iframe widgets that hide content.
- **Make core pricing content server-rendered and crawlable**: Move pricing tables and self-service plan details out of JavaScript-only or click-to-reveal elements so AI engines can read them, as Profound did after finding its own pricing was partly hidden from bots.
- **Audit what AI says about your pricing across engines**: Run prompts like 'Evaluate [Company] on pricing' in ChatGPT, Google AI Mode, Gemini, Perplexity, and others, and track how often the answers are negative or inaccurate.
- **Build a pricing answer stack, not just a pricing page**: Manage the third-party sources AI relies on, such as review sites and forums like Reddit, so that accurate pricing information is available wherever AI looks.
- **Clarify pricing where you have none**: For companies without public prices, publish enough structure (plan names, what's included, price ranges) to keep AI from filling the gap with inflated third-party estimates that could scare buyers or weaken your negotiating position.
