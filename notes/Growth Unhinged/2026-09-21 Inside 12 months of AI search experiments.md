# Inside 12 months of AI search experiments

**Source**: [Growth Unhinged](https://www.growthunhinged.com/p/hubspot-ai-search-experiments)
**Author**: Kyle Poyar (featuring Aja Frost) | **Date**: Sep 21, 2026

---

## Summary

HubSpot's blog traffic collapsed after Google's late-2024 algorithm change, so it ran a fast experimental team to build an AI search strategy. Through weekly tests, it learned that its own data should validate tactics like llms.txt, that content must be structured for bots as well as humans, and that AI-driven leads rose 1,850% by year end.

## Key Takeaways

- **Validate AI search tactics with your own data before scaling them.** HubSpot's llms.txt test showed zero bot visits and no recognition, and industry hype was not evidence.
- **Expect a lag between crawls and citations.** HubSpot's bots crawled its pages first, citations stayed around 16% for a while, and then 92% of its 141 vertical pages were cited.
- **Use AI-generated content only with a QA loop.** Log every hallucination and jargon issue, then iterate the prompt until accuracy and readability are acceptable for humans and Googlebot.
- **Fix wrong AI pricing answers by publishing crawlable pricing content.** Because AI bots can't render JavaScript, they pulled outdated third-party pricing, so HubSpot wrote structured blog posts describing each product's pricing.
- **Sort content into human-only, bot-only, or both.** This framework helps decide where to spend effort, since some content serves humans, some serves bots, and some serves both.
