# Startups become what they hunt

**Source**: [Growth Unhinged](https://www.growthunhinged.com/p/startups-become-what-they-hunt)
**Author**: Kyle Poyar | **Date**: Oct 23, 2026

---

## Summary

Analyzing 1,043 SaaS and AI companies that reached $10k MRR, the article argues that a startup's initial target customer deal size largely determines its long-term trajectory, retention, and growth. Companies that hunt deer (about 10,000 customers at $10k/year) grow fastest, and migrating between segments rarely fixes early mistakes.

## Key Takeaways

- **Choose your target deal size deliberately from day one.** Early customer segment choices shape retention and growth for years, so treat pricing and ICP as foundational strategy rather than a detail to adjust later.
- **Be wary of the mouse and rabbit paths unless you can sustain high churn.** Mouse and rabbit hunters at $10k MRR had gross revenue retention as low as 24.6% and 31.6%, versus 70.5% for deer hunters.
- **Target deer (mid-market buyers at roughly $1,000/month) if you want durable growth.** Companies that stayed deer hunters grew about 22% year-over-year, far faster than the 2% to 5% seen in other groups.
- **If you must migrate, move upmarket one step at a time.** The only migration that reliably helped was rabbit to deer; moving downmarket, such as rabbit to mouse, correlated with negative growth.
- **Pair PLG or low-price entry with a plan to sell up.** Self-serve users can be upsold later, but only once you have usage data to sharpen your ICP and build a repeatable sales motion.
