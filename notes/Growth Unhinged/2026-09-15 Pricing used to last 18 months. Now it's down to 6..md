# Pricing used to last 18 months. Now it's down to 6.

**Source**: [Growth Unhinged](https://www.growthunhinged.com/p/saas-pricing-out-of-date)
**Author**: Kyle Poyar | **Date**: Sep 15, 2026

---

## Summary

Pricing models now go stale in about 6 months instead of 18, so teams need a regular diagnostic to decide when to change pricing. Kyle Poyar argues that four questions, starting with what the business is optimizing for, reveal whether a pricing model still fits.

## Key Takeaways

- **Align leadership on objectives before touching price:** Have each executive independently rate the 3-5 pricing trade-offs (acquisition vs. revenue vs. profitability, new logos vs. expansion, predictable vs. upside revenue, simple vs. customized), then compare the results in the next exec meeting to set a North Star.
- **Target losing about 20% of deals on price:** Track the share of deals lost due to pricing. Losing close to 0% suggests prices are too low, and losing 40% or more signals a market shift that needs a pricing response.
- **Recognize the limits of each pricing model:** Flat-fee subscriptions block upsell, seat-based pricing is not future-proof, hybrid models are hard to explain, and token pricing is commoditizing, so audit your current model against these failure modes.
- **Use a willingness-to-pay survey to test changes:** Before repricing, run a structured survey to learn what customers value and will pay, using the Claude skill for designing one (available to paid subscribers).
