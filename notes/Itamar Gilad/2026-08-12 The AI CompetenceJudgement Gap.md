# The AI Competence/Judgement Gap

**Source**: [Itamar Gilad](https://itamargilad.com/artificial-competence/)
**Author**: Itamar Gilad | **Date**: Aug 12, 2026

---

## Summary

AI enables people to perform tasks beyond their expertise, but this creates a dangerous gap where people can do work they're not equipped to judge, making them vulnerable to overconfidence biases and errors.

## Key Takeaways

- **Recognize the competence/judgment gap**: AI lets you perform tasks at medium quality that you can't properly evaluate, creating blind spots where you may produce harmful or subpar work without realizing it.
- **Use AI as a judge, not just a tool**: Deploy LLMs to review your AI-generated outputs in domains where you lack expertise—they can catch 89% of harmful changes, dramatically outperforming human review alone.
- **Build expert review workflows into your process**: Don't rely on self-policing or cognitive discipline; implement mandatory expert review stages for AI-generated work in critical domains like security, finance, or legal.
- **Invest strategically in skill development**: Rather than becoming a jack-of-all-trades, focus learning on high-impact domains where you regularly generate AI outputs that could cause real harm if misjudged.
- **Watch for approval fatigue**: Establish AI review governance structures that prevent cognitive surrender—humans stop paying attention after seeing many approvals, so automate the judgment itself rather than relying on human gatekeeping.
