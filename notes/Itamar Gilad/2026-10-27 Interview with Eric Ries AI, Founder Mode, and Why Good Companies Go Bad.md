# Interview with Eric Ries: AI, Founder Mode, and Why Good Companies Go Bad

**Source**: [Itamar Gilad](https://itamargilad.com/eric-ries-incorruptible/)
**Author**: Itamar Gilad (featuring Eric Ries) | **Date**: Oct 27, 2026

---

## Summary

In this interview, Eric Ries argues that AI makes validated learning more important, because cheaper building raises the cost of wrong turns, and that the Lean Startup loop of build, measure, and learn must stay balanced. He also argues that companies can resist short-term financial pressure through governance choices and small "islands of freedom" that protect mission-focused work.

## Key Takeaways

- **Keep the full build-measure-learn loop running.** Faster building doesn't shorten the loop by itself, so teams should check whether they are still measuring and learning from real customers, not just shipping features.
- **Don't replace real customers with simulated ones.** Using AI-generated feedback as a stand-in for talking to or selling to actual customers is risky. Validate with real users and real purchases.
- **Treat validation as building real things, not as separate research.** Expose real customers to minimum viable products early instead of running a research phase before building.
- **Start change with small islands of freedom.** When the wider organization is dysfunctional, a few committed people can build a protected bubble, then grow it by pulling in middle managers tied to the specific project.
- **Redefine value beyond profit.** Look for hidden assets such as trustworthiness, integrity, safety, and open source, which can strengthen morale, innovation, and long-term value without changing the business model.
