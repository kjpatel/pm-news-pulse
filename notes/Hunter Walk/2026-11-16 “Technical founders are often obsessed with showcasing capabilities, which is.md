## Summary

Dakota McKenzie argues that technical founders should win a clear 'technical win' first, then learn to make their product's value legible to buyers, since customer enthusiasm and large contracts can mask a lack of real commercial traction. He also contends that founders should build GTM skills themselves rather than outsource them or hire executives too early.

## Key Takeaways

- **Separate enthusiasm from commercial progress.** Don't treat a big logo's meeting or excitement about the technology as a deal; ask what purchasing process and budget must be in place before counting it as progress.
- **Make value legible to both technologists and buyers.** Translate technical capabilities into business outcomes a buyer can evaluate, and test whether a non-technical stakeholder can explain why they should pay.
- **Hire sales leadership only when selling is repeatable.** Bring in an executive once the founder understands the sales motion well enough to describe it and the opportunity exceeds what they can personally execute.
- **Walk away from deals that turn you into a services business.** Before accepting a large contract, check whether it accelerates adoption of your core technology or requires rebuilding the product around requirements you don't intend to support.
- **Learn GTM yourself rather than outsourcing it.** Founders, including CEOs, should personally understand how each go-to-market function operates so they can judge hires and decisions.
