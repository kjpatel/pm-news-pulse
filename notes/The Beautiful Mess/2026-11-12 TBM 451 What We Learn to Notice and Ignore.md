# TBM 451: What We Learn to Notice and Ignore

**Source**: [The Beautiful Mess](https://cutlefish.substack.com/p/tbm-451-what-we-learn-to-notice-and)
**Author**: John Cutler | **Date**: Nov 12, 2026

---

## Summary

People respond differently to contradictions between what leaders say and what organizations do, and those responses often reflect childhood experiences and personal meaning rather than simple differences in character. Recognizing these hidden origins helps collaborators avoid turning assumptions about others' behavior into stories about their intentions or competence.

## Key Takeaways

- **Treat workplace contradictions as data, not drama.** When leadership statements don't match incentives or decisions, note the gap and ask what it reveals about priorities before deciding how to respond.
- **Check your assumptions before labeling a colleague.** When someone challenges, delays, or disengages, consider alternative drivers like a need for coherence, a need for momentum, or a read of how the organization really works before judging their motives.
- **Ask what the other person's behavior is protecting.** Resistance may be an effort to protect a sense of reality, and impatience may reflect a learned need to keep things moving, so address that underlying need directly.
- **Notice your own relationship to incoherence.** Reflect on whether your discomfort with contradictions comes from your past experiences, and decide whether you want to act on it or work around it in a given situation.
- **Keep the 'voices in your head' in check.** When you catch yourself framing colleagues as deflectors or power-hungry antagonists, pause and replace that story with specific, observable behaviors you can discuss openly.
