# TBM 450: The Loss of Positive Friction

**Source**: [The Beautiful Mess](https://cutlefish.substack.com/p/tbm-450-the-loss-of-positive-friction)
**Author**: John Cutler | **Date**: Nov 07, 2026

---

## Summary

The article argues that a constraint can simultaneously enable and limit, so teams should evaluate constraints by both their benefits and costs rather than treating them as purely good or bad. It connects this idea to the loss of positive friction, the useful resistance that drives thinking and quality.

## Key Takeaways

- **Map both sides of each constraint.** For every constraint your team works under, list what it makes possible and what it prevents, then decide which side matters more for the current goal.
- **Protect productive friction.** Identify steps where effort forces clarity, such as writing a short design rationale or debating trade-offs, and keep them even when a tool offers to remove them.
- **Question automation before adopting it.** Before automating a task, ask what thinking, learning, or shared understanding the friction currently produces, and whether that output is worth keeping.
- **Revisit constraints periodically.** A constraint that enabled focus last quarter may now limit experimentation, so schedule regular reviews to decide whether to keep, loosen, or remove it.
- **Make trade-offs explicit with the team.** When introducing or removing a constraint, state the expected enabling and limiting effects out loud so everyone can see what is being gained and lost.
