# TBM 447: Why We Sabotage Our Careers

**Author**: John Cutler | **Date**: Oct 25, 2026

## Summary

The article argues that people often undermine their own careers in ways that persist despite effort, and that simply trying harder at self-improvement doesn't always fix the problem. The subtitle is the only available text, so this summary is inferred from the title and subtitle.

## Key Takeaways

- **Look for patterns in your own behavior before adding effort.** Review the last three career setbacks or missed opportunities and note what you did or avoided each time, rather than assuming you need more discipline.
- **Question whether the fix is structural rather than personal.** Before committing to a self-improvement plan, ask whether the problem sits in your environment, incentives, or role design, and whether a change to those would do more than willpower.
- **Notice where you hold yourself back on purpose.** Identify situations where you consistently downplay your work, avoid visibility, or stop short of a stretch role, and examine what fear or belief drives that choice.
- **Seek outside feedback on the blind spots you can't see.** Ask a trusted peer or manager to name one behavior that repeatedly works against your goals, and act on one specific change based on their answer.
- **Replace generic resolutions with a single testable experiment.** Choose one behavior to change for the next 30 days, define how you'll know whether it helped, and review the results before adding anything else.
