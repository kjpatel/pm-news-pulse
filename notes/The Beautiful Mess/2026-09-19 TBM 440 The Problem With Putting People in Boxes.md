# TBM 440: The Problem With Putting People in Boxes

**Author**: John Cutler | **Date**: Sep 19, 2026

## Summary

The article opens with the author's long-standing skepticism toward personality and 'strength' tests used at work, suggesting that sorting people into fixed categories can be limiting. The excerpt provided is only a single sentence, so the full argument is not available.

## Key Takeaways

- **Treat personality and strength labels as hypotheses, not verdicts.** Use them as a starting point for conversation rather than a basis for hiring, promotion, or team placement decisions.
- **Ask what a person does in specific situations, not what box they fit.** Replace 'she's a strategic thinker' with observed examples of how she approached a recent problem.
- **Audit where test results are driving decisions.** List any staffing, role assignment, or team design choices that rely on personality or strengths assessments, and check whether direct evidence supports them.
- **Give people room to grow beyond their labeled strengths.** Assign stretch work outside the category a test assigned them, and see how they perform before deciding what they are good at.
- **Use team discussions to build shared context instead of profiles.** Have people explain how they prefer to work and what they're trying to learn, which tends to be more accurate and more useful than a fixed type.
