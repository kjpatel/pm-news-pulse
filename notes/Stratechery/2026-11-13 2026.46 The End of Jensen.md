## Summary

This week's Stratechery roundup centers on the sudden ouster of Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang after a 32-year tenure, with Ben Thompson and collaborators examining what led to it and what it means for Nvidia, the tech industry, and U.S. policy. The issue also covers Intel's struggles versus AMD, the NBA, and a sponsored note on agentic commerce.

## Key Takeaways

- **Track governance and government relations as strategic risks:** Thompson's coverage argues the real danger to Huang was governance and national-security scrutiny rather than the market alone, so leaders should monitor board and regulatory exposure alongside competitive threats.
- **Compare Intel and AMD to see where enterprise demand is going:** AMD is capitalizing on enterprise AI demand while Intel faces flat consumer sales, so evaluate your own vendor and chip bets by whether they serve enterprise AI workloads.
- **Plan for leadership transitions at critical suppliers:** Nvidia's abrupt CEO change shows how quickly a dominant supplier's direction can shift, so build contingency plans and multi-vendor options for key infrastructure.
- **Give agents bounded spending authority:** Stripe's Link model of approving a set budget, allowing price-change adjustments, and offering purchase protection shows how to let AI agents transact safely, which is worth copying if you are building agentic commerce.
- **Watch aggregation dynamics in AI-transformed platforms:** The Roblox interview suggests AI can let platforms act as both aggregator and enabler for creators, so assess whether your platform can capture value by supporting developers rather than competing with them.
