# 2026.44: At Long Last…

**Source**: [Stratechery](https://stratechery.com/2026/at-long-last/)
**Author**: Ben Thompson | **Date**: Oct 30, 2026

---

## Summary

This week's Stratechery roundup argues that Anthropic's S-1 finally provides real numbers for the economics of frontier AI labs, showing a very profitable core business that competition and investor dilution could still erode. It also argues that AI pressure may fragment the global internet into more localized, and potentially better, products and services.

## Key Takeaways

- **Read the S-1 for unit economics, not headlines:** Use Anthropic's filing to test claims about frontier lab profitability against the core business's margins, and separate the profitable product from the costs of compute and competition.
- **Model dilution alongside revenue growth:** Before judging AI company valuations, estimate how continued funding rounds and IPO share issuance will dilute investors even if the underlying business keeps growing.
- **Expect competition to absorb AI profits:** Assume that as models commoditize, price competition can capture much of the margin, so build pricing and positioning that do not depend on frontier-model scarcity.
- **Plan for a more localized internet:** Consider how the decline of the open, globally shared internet could push users toward smaller communities and local products, and evaluate which of your distribution channels depend on that open web.
- **Use the Meta and Muse debate as a test case:** When assessing a company's large AI capital spending, check whether investors' belief in the product justifies the compute commitment, and track whether that belief is being tested by actual adoption.
