## Summary

Jensen Huang's resignation as Nvidia's CEO was less about his personal conduct than about Nvidia's uneasy position as a politically sensitive defense-adjacent company, which Huang never adjusted to. The article argues that Nvidia's CUDA, systems, and supply-chain moats were already under pressure, and that its founder-centric, startup-like structure may now collapse without him.

## Key Takeaways

- **Stress-test software moats against LLM-driven development:** Assume that any moat built on software libraries or APIs can erode quickly as LLMs make code cheap to write, and prioritize moats in systems, integration, or hard-to-copy operations instead.
- **Audit key-person dependency before it becomes a crisis:** Identify which relationships, decisions, and institutional knowledge depend on one leader, and document succession plans and decision rights now rather than after an exit.
- **Treat handshake-based supplier relationships as fragile:** Formalize critical supplier commitments with written contracts where possible, since trust tied to one individual may not survive a leadership change.
- **Plan for political and regulatory exposure as a business risk:** Build relationships in Washington and other key capitals before controls arrive, and avoid minimum-compliance strategies that only satisfy the letter of rules, since regulators and the public may judge by the spirit.
- **Be wary of founder-led organizations that only act like startups:** Check whether your organization has real checks and balances on its leader, since speed gained from minimal management can also remove the oversight that prevents catastrophic mistakes.
