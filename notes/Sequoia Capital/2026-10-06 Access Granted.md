# Access Granted

**Source**: [Sequoia Capital](https://www.sequoiacap.com/article/access-granted)
**Author**: Sequoia Capital (featuring Jake Stauch) | **Date**: Oct 06, 2026

---

## Summary

Serval, founded by Jake Stauch after a failed neuroscience startup, argues that AI can move IT professionals out of repetitive ticket-taking and form-filling and into roles as builders of tools and protectors of essential data. The piece frames this shift as a chance to change the long-standing Dilbert-style image of IT work.

## Key Takeaways

- **Audit where IT time goes today.** Identify the repetitive work, such as ticket triage, form-filling, and access-granting, that consumes IT capacity and could be automated by an AI-driven service management platform.
- **Redirect IT staff toward building.** Reassign freed capacity to building internal tools and automating workflows, so IT becomes a builder of solutions rather than a responder to requests.
- **Treat IT as a guardian of essential data.** As AI handles routine requests, put IT professionals in charge of data protection, access governance, and security oversight, which become higher-value responsibilities.
- **Validate market fit before scaling.** Stauch's startup failed after years of building on an assumed market that proved far narrower than expected, so test demand with real customers early and watch acquisition metrics closely.
- **Build a culture that rewards persistence and candor.** Stauch's path reflects the value of intense focus and direct feedback, so teams should make it safe to admit when a bet is not working and to pivot quickly.
