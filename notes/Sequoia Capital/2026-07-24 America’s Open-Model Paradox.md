# America’s Open-Model Paradox

**Source**: [Sequoia Capital](https://www.sequoiacap.com/article/americas-open-model-paradox)
**Author**: Sequoia Capital (featuring Dean Meyer and Konstantine Buhler) | **Date**: Jul 24, 2026

---

## Summary

The U.S. faces a critical paradox where Western AI companies depend on Chinese open-source models for capability transfer because American frontier labs lack a legal mechanism to share their own advanced models domestically. Without establishing a direct path for lawful capability transfer, the West risks technological dependence on China while Chinese labs gain a structural advantage through distillation and post-training on Western frontier models.

## Key Takeaways

- **Establish controlled teacher access**: Frontier labs should be able to sell structured training rights to qualifying Western and allied companies, allowing lawful capability transfer with metering, auditing, and appropriate restrictions on sensitive capabilities.
- **Create a domestic distillation route**: American companies need a legal path to absorb frontier capabilities developed domestically rather than relying on Chinese open models as teachers—currently 69% of new open-model adaptations use Qwen.
- **Audit security risks in foreign models**: Open-weight models don't guarantee freedom from backdoors or poisoned training data; Western builders should evaluate whether supply-chain risks are acceptable for defense, infrastructure, and intelligence applications.
- **Raise enforcement costs for foreign distillation**: Strengthen identity verification, access controls, and proxy disruption to make covert capability extraction harder while debating whether federal AI contract access should be conditioned on using domestic models.
- **Question the current equilibrium**: The West shouldn't accept a system where it creates frontier models, capabilities leak through Chinese open models, and Western builders then depend on those Chinese models for affordability and sovereignty.
