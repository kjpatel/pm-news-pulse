# America’s Open-Model Paradox

**Source**: [Sequoia Capital](https://sequoiacap.com/article/americas-open-model-paradox/)
**Author**: Sequoia Capital (featuring Dean Meyer and Konstantine Buhler) | **Date**: Jul 24, 2026

---

## Summary

The U.S. faces a strategic vulnerability where Western AI companies depend on Chinese open-source models for post-training and capability transfer, while lacking a legal domestic pathway to distill their own frontier models—forcing them to choose between covert reliance on Chinese models or independent development.

## Key Takeaways

- **Establish controlled teacher access**: Frontier labs should create a legal, priced mechanism for Western companies to access structured training rights from American frontier models, with access trailing the frontier and limited to verified companies to prevent illicit foreign distillation.
- **Reduce Chinese model dependency**: Without a domestic distillation route, Western AI startups will continue relying on Chinese open weights (Qwen, Kimi, GLM) as their primary base for post-training, synthetic data generation, and capability advancement—creating a structural disadvantage.
- **Address security risks in open weights**: Chinese open-source models present unauditable supply-chain risks for defense and critical infrastructure applications, as weights alone cannot guarantee the absence of embedded backdoors or trigger-dependent behaviors.
- **Accelerate Western base model development**: Continue investment in American pre-trained models (Reflection, TML, Nemotron), but recognize that stronger pre-training alone doesn't solve the teacher problem without legal capability transfer mechanisms.
- **Enforce distillation restrictions while developing alternatives**: Increase enforcement costs for foreign distillation through identity verification and access controls, but only as a temporary measure until a voluntary domestic market for capability transfer emerges.
