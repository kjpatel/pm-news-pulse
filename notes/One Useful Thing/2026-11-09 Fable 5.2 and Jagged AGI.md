# Fable 5.2 and Jagged AGI

**Source**: [One Useful Thing](https://www.oneusefulthing.org/p/fable-52-and-jagged-agi)
**Author**: Ethan Mollick | **Date**: Nov 09, 2026

---

## Summary

Ethan Mollick argues that Anthropic's Claude Fable 5.2 is "almost" Artificial General Intelligence, or what he calls Jagged AGI: it is strong across many computer-based intellectual tasks, but its gaps are now subtler, involving taste, judgment, and running long-term business operations rather than simple errors. He tests this through hardware hacking, business launch, humanities course recreation, and original research.

## Key Takeaways

- **Test AI on open-ended, multi-step work, not just benchmarks.** Mollick's one-shot prompts (a hardware hack, a business plan, a research paper) show where capability really lies, so run similar real tasks in your own domain before judging a model.
- **Expect jagged performance in taste and judgment.** Fable produced accurate but joyless output and confidently wrong literary readings without noticing, so build human review checkpoints for aesthetic, interpretive, and quality-sensitive work.
- **Provide specific feedback to close quality gaps quickly.** Mollick's fixes took minutes once he named the problem (e.g., "a brutal slog" needed to become beautiful), so give concrete critiques rather than accepting a first draft.
- **Don't hand AI unsupervised control of money or operations yet.** Fable's business plan was plausible, but the ongoing infrastructure and sales execution were unproven, so keep a human in the loop for spending and operational decisions.
- **Use agent critics and fact-checkers to verify output.** Mollick's humanities project used critic agents and fact-checkers alongside human cross-checks, a practical pattern for catching errors in long AI-generated work.
