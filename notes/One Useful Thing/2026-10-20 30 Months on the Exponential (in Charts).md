# 30 Months on the Exponential (in Charts)

**Source**: [One Useful Thing](https://www.oneusefulthing.org/p/30-months-on-the-exponential-in-charts)
**Author**: Ethan Mollick | **Date**: Oct 20, 2026

---

## Summary

Ethan Mollick uses charts and his new book's themes to argue that AI progress is exponential but uneven, with capabilities doubling on task length while costs fall sharply. He contends that work is shifting from chatbots to delegated agents, which requires a new way of working, the 'conjurer' role, alongside the centaur and cyborg modes.

## Key Takeaways

- **Re-evaluate the tasks you assume AI can't do.** Capability is rising quickly on some benchmarks (e.g., long-task duration, math) while staying weak on others (e.g., long-form writing), so test specific workflows rather than relying on old impressions.
- **Adopt the 'conjurer' mode for delegated work.** Instead of staying in the loop on every step, summon agents with clear goals and general guidance, then carefully evaluate what they return.
- **Expect costs to fall and budget for more experimentation.** Benchmark tasks that once cost cents now cost a fraction of a cent, so run more options and iterations rather than rationing AI use.
- **Watch for loss of control as agents take on more work.** Review what agents actually do to reach results, since delegation makes it easier to miss errors or unintended steps.
- **Build your human advantage around deep knowledge, wide knowledge, taste, and agency.** Decide deliberately when to hand work to AI, and when to avoid it entirely.
