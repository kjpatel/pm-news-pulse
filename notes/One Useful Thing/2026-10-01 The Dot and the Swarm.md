# The Dot and the Swarm

**Source**: [One Useful Thing](https://www.oneusefulthing.org/p/the-dot-and-the-swarm)
**Author**: Ethan Mollick | **Date**: Oct 01, 2026

---

## Summary

The author admits he was wrong to think managing groups of AI agents would require careful human-designed organizational structures. Like earlier prompting and retrieval systems, coordination is another task that more capable models learn to handle themselves, as shown by dots, Clawlike personal agents, and OpenAI's Navier-Stokes swarm.

## Key Takeaways

- **Delegate the organizing itself to the model.** Set the goal and a rough framework, then let the model decide how many agents to spin up and how they should split the work, instead of designing the org chart yourself.
- **Describe outcomes and roles, not step-by-step plans.** A few sentences sketching teams, such as brainstormers, researchers, and reviewers, was enough to produce a much larger, self-organized structure.
- **Connect personal agents to your accounts and let them learn from your messages.** Give them access to email, calendars, and financial records so they pick up context without you typing it out, and expect them to proactively flag problems.
- **Review agent output for errors you made, not just errors the agent made.** Personal agents can catch mistakes like a wrong project number on a permit and draft corrections, so build a habit of checking what they flag.
- **Use delegation-enabled modes when the task is complex.** Selecting a high-capability mode signals that the model can spawn sub-agents, so use it for open-ended work like brainstorming or research.
